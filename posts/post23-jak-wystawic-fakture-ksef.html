How to issue a KSeF invoice — step by step

The first KSeF invoice raises the most questions: how to log in, what to fill in, and how to know the document "went through." Below is a practical step-by-step guide — on the free government tools — plus the most common cases (correction, prepayment) and the point where it's worth automating.

1. What you need before you start

To issue an invoice in KSeF you need two things: a way to authenticate and access to the system.

  • Authentication — usually Profil Zaufany, a qualified signature, a qualified seal, or a KSeF token generated in the system.
  • Permissions — the business owner has them automatically; an employee or accounting office must be granted them in KSeF.
  • Invoice data — buyer's NIP, line items, VAT rates, dates. The KSeF format is the structured FA(3) schema.

New to the rules? Start with: KSeF in 2026: timeline and practical preparation ↗ and Free KSeF invoicing software: what are your options ↗.

2. Step by step in Aplikacja Podatnika KSeF (free)

The simplest free route is the government Aplikacja Podatnika KSeF:

  • Step 1. Log in to the app (e.g. via Profil Zaufany) and choose the company context (your NIP).
  • Step 2. Choose "Issue invoice" and the form based on the FA(3) schema.
  • Step 3. Fill in the data: seller, buyer (NIP), line items, VAT rates, sale and issue dates.
  • Step 4. Review the summary — the system validates required fields and basic logic.
  • Step 5. Send the invoice. Once accepted, KSeF assigns it a KSeF number and generates UPO (official confirmation of receipt) — proof the document was accepted correctly.

The issue date of a structured invoice is, as a rule, the date it was sent to KSeF. Keep the KSeF number and UPO — they confirm the document flow.

3. Common cases

  • Correction invoice — also issued in KSeF, referencing the KSeF number of the original invoice.
  • Prepayment invoice — supported by the FA(3) schema; remember to link it to the final invoice.
  • Sale to a private individual (consumer) — special rules apply here; check the current guidelines before treating it as a rule.
  • Flat-rate / small firm — the issuing mechanism is the same; the scope of data and settlements differs.

4. When to automate (instead of typing by hand)

Manual issuing in the government app is fine for a few invoices. When there are many — or they arise from orders, warehouse or e-commerce — it's worth having the invoice created automatically in your system and sent to KSeF on its own. That's where programs come in: paid packages or free open-source.

A few real open-source options — with pros and cons (optional, no strings attached):
  • MyCompany — a free open-source ERP (Apache-2.0) on the lsFusion platform.
    Pro: it's a full ERP — invoices arise from orders/warehouse, process automation; the public demo includes invoice issuing. Con: as a platform it usually needs configuration for your processes — a higher entry bar than a simple app.
  • Dolibarr — a mature open-source ERP/CRM with a large community.
    Pro: plenty of ready modules, proven in thousands of firms. Con: Polish KSeF support is usually via third-party modules, not out of the box.
  • Invoice Ninja — a lightweight open-source invoicing app.
    Pro: simple and fast to start, focused on invoices and payments. Con: not a full ERP; no KSeF-specific features.

KSeF support varies between tools — worth checking each one. These are just examples.

5. Most common mistakes — what to check

  • wrong buyer NIP or a typo in the data — validation catches it, but slows you down;
  • missing permissions for an employee/office — the invoice "won't send";
  • KSeF number / UPO not saved — harder to confirm the flow later;
  • treating KSeF like a PDF — it's structured XML, not an attachment.

6. Summary

Issuing a KSeF invoice comes down to: authenticate → fill in FA(3) → send → receive the KSeF number and UPO. You can do it for free in Aplikacja Podatnika KSeF; at larger scale it's worth having invoices created automatically in your system — paid or open-source.
